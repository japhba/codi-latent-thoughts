# Blinded systematicity review — Judge 2 (Sonnet)

Scope: `packet.json` contains 28 problems. Reader A is annotated on 20 of them (12 shared with B: g5036, g5044, g5052, g5056, g5060, g5068, g5088, g5096, g5100, g5104, g5144, g5148; plus 8 A-only: g5082, g5089, g5005, g5043, g5115, g5099, g5087, g5054). Reader B is annotated on 20 (the same 12 shared, plus 8 B-only: g5500, g5412, g5612, g5324, g5544, g5276, g5368, g5672). All claims below are read directly off the text at the cited uid/step; I did not infer or speculate about what produced the text.

## 1. Reader A systematicities

Reader A's text is written in the register of a next-token-prediction rationale for a piece of *academic, statistical, or consumer-review prose* — never in the register of a grade-school arithmetic word problem, and never referencing the actual entities of the problem it is nominally describing (no restaurants, cookies, marbles, stickers, turtles, lizards, or lego blocks appear anywhere in A's 80 step-texts). Two recurring sub-registers cover essentially all of A's output: (i) a "results section" narrative — regression coefficients, confidence intervals, p-values, poll percentages, "the authors found that the effect... is [decimal]" — and (ii) a pipe-delimited "structured table" narrative — rows of r-values, correlation coefficients, or probability ranks. Every one of the 20 annotated A problems sits in exactly one of these two buckets for *all four* of its own steps (never switching bucket mid-problem): e.g. g5060 and g5148 stay in the table bucket at steps 100/200/300, while g5036, g5044, g5052, g5056, g5068, g5082, g5087, g5088, g5089, g5096, g5100, g5104, g5115, g5099, g5144, g5054, g5005, g5043 all stay in the results-narrative bucket throughout.

Topicality is essentially zero: none of the 80 A texts mention the subject matter of the problem they annotate. Value recovery is also essentially zero: scanning every A text for the gold_chain/gold_answer digits of its own problem (e.g. 50/75/525 for g5036, 60/48 for g5096, 90/690/69/621 for g5100), none appear — A's numbers are all generic exemplars of the statistics genre ("62%," "0.35," "0.999," "1,234 Games") that never coincide with a gold value.

The most striking systematicity is verbatim cross-problem repetition. A's step_0 text for g5036 ("A restaurant serves 25 customers..."), g5100 ("...road bike... $600 before the increase"), and g5104 ("...Dustin grabs snacks...") is byte-identical: *"Statistical results pattern: each finding reports a percentage with confidence interval, e.g., '62% (95% CI: 58%–66%)' — the next tokens must complete this same format... Final token '62% (' opens a parenthetical confidence interval, immediately requiring a percentage value followed by '% CI: X%–X%' to match the established statistical reporting pattern."* Likewise, g5036's step_300 text ("...the magnitude of the effect on the 2010–2012 panel; a specific numerical value (likely a decimal like '0.39' or similar) is immediately expected") is identical to g5100's step_300 text word for word. The table-bucket problems show the same effect: g5060 step_100 and g5148 step_100 open with the identical sentence *"Structured table pattern: rows follow consistent format of `|r||coefficient||coefficient||coefficient|`, with r values incrementing (0.00, 0.01, 0.05, 0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 1.00)"* before diverging slightly. g5089 (step_0) and g5005 (step_0) both open *"The article follows a structured sports news format, presenting study findings with statistics and expert quotes, building toward a complete summary of results"* and both anchor on "10.5% of the 1,000 male [athletes/players]." g5115 (step_0) and g5099 (step_0) share, verbatim, *"Statistical results pattern: each finding pairs a group with a specific percentage, e.g., '67% of non-Black participants,' '62% of Black participants,' '65% of participants who are not Black.'"* These are four different underlying problems (a bike-shop discount, a snack budget, running mileage, turtles and lettuce) producing near- or fully-identical descriptions.

From step 0 to step 300, A's surface style narrows rather than becomes more grounded: step_0 draws from a wider range of journalistic/consumer flavors (sports polls, a casino review in g5060/g5148, a bingo FAQ in g5043, an anniversary-compilation blurb — note that field belongs to reader B, see below), while steps 100–300 converge overwhelmingly onto the "OLS regression coefficient" sub-flavor of the results-narrative bucket, regardless of which problem is being annotated. This is a homogenization of an already off-topic register, not an emergence of topicality.

## 2. Reader B systematicities

Reader B's text is almost always in the register of a grade-school arithmetic word problem being solved step by step ("Mathematical calculation in progress...," "The problem establishes...," "Final token 'is' opens a declarative sentence requiring a specific numerical answer"). This genre-level topicality is much higher than A's: of the 80 B texts, only a handful break register — g5544 step_0 pivots into a coding-exercise frame (*"the answer is 55... the prompt structure follows a coding exercise format: problem statement followed by a code implementation... 'Total socks:' is a label/heading... likely `def total_socks`"*), g5672 step_0 pivots into a retail/e-commerce product-description frame (*"structured product listing format (material, origin, care, sizing) signals a retail/e-commerce product description genre"*), and g5368 step_0 drifts into circle/area geometry (*"total area 314, one part 157"*) rather than the weight problem at hand. g5324 is a distinct failure mode: from step_100 onward its text degenerates into a mechanical repeated-fraction listing (*"1/3+1/4+1/5+1/6+1/7+...+1/49+1/50+1/5"* at step_100, a similarly repeating sum at step_200, and by step_300 a long non-semantic run of "+1"s), i.e. incoherent rather than merely off-topic.

Despite almost never using the correct nouns of the actual cover story (it invents socks, coins, tickets, drinks, eggs, fish tanks, and pairs in place of restaurants, marbles, lego blocks, etc.), B's invented arithmetic frequently reuses the *real numbers* of the problem it is annotating, and sometimes reaches the exact gold answer. Representative exact matches: for g5044 (Dean's pay, gold_chain ['48','6','12'], answer 60) step_200 states *"The specific numerical context demands a precise dollar amount completing the arithmetic: 12×6/2 = 72, but since one day is excluded, the expected score is 60"* — reusing "6" and "12" and landing on the literal gold answer "60," inside an invented test-score story. For g5148 (Adam/Hannah, answer 15) B reconstructs the exact real equation three steps running: step_100 *"the answer must be 45 ÷ 3 = 15"*, step_200 *"the numerical answer '45' or '15' (since books = total ÷ 3)"*, step_300 *"45 = 3 × girls, so the solution must show the number of girls is 15."* For g5544 (apple tree, 18/day, answer 126) B's step_300 states *"the problem has established specific numerical values (7 days, 18 eggs/day) that must be combined, so the answer will be 7 × 18 = 126 eggs"* — every operand and the final answer are exact, inside a story about eggs rather than apples. For g5412 (Lego blocks, answer 50) step_200 gives *"the answer must be 50"* and step_300 gives *"the answer must be 500 - 150 - 300 - 200 = 50"*, both landing on the exact gold answer while also reusing the real operands 150 and 300. For g5068 (parking lot, answer 10) step_200 and step_300 both land on *"the answer must be '10'"* while reusing the problem's real "4 rows" and "10" slots. For g5672 (Jessica's apples) step_200 reconstructs *"3*8 = 24"* (the real first sub-computation) and step_300 reconstructs *"3×8 − 2×5"* (both real sub-computations' operands), without ever combining them into the correct final 14.

This produces a systematicity that cuts the other way from A: B's *cover story* is essentially never stable or correct, but B's *specific numeric operands*, once they surface, tend to recur within a problem — 45/3/15 across three steps of g5148, 6/12 across three steps of g5044 and of g5612, 3×8 across three steps of g5672. Some problems never surface real numbers at all (g5052, g5276 — which is consistently wrong by close to a factor of 10 at every step, e.g. step_300 *"24 students... 2/3*15 + 1/4*12 = 4"* against a true total of 240 — and g5324's degeneration).

## 3. A vs B contrast

The sharpest contrast is genre: A never leaves an academic/statistics/review register, B is almost always inside an arithmetic word-problem register. On the one problem pair where this is easiest to see side by side, g5148 (Adam has three times as many books as Hannah), A step_100 reads *"Structured table pattern: rows follow consistent format of `|r||coefficient||coefficient||coefficient|`... Final token 'and is' opens a numerical value clause mid-cell, immediately requiring a decimal coefficient (likely '0.900' or similar)"*, while B step_100 for the same problem reads *"The problem establishes a ratio relationship: 'three times the number of friends' means the smaller group has 4 times the smaller number, so the answer must be 45 ÷ 3 = 15."* Only B's text has any relationship to what the problem actually asks.

The second sharp contrast is value recovery. Across all 80 A texts I found zero instances of a gold intermediate or gold answer appearing as a number. Across B's texts I found numerous instances, e.g. g5044 step_200 (*"the expected score is 60"*, exact gold answer) and g5068 step_300 (*"the answer must be '10' (or '10 cells')"*, exact gold answer, reusing the real "4×10" grid). A never produces anything comparable on the same two problems: g5044 A-step_200 is *"the authors found that the effect of the study on the proportion of people who smoke is [decimal]"* and g5068 A-step_300 is about *"the effect of the number of years of experience on the logit of being a victim of a crime."*

A third contrast is the *kind* of repetition each reader shows. A repeats itself verbatim across *different* problems (g5036, g5100, and g5104's step_0 texts are byte-identical, as are g5115 and g5099's step_0 texts), indicating its output is largely independent of which problem it is nominally attached to. B does not repeat verbatim across problems, but it does reuse a small set of *cover-story motifs* (e.g. "socks" opens both g5544 step_0 and g5672 step_0, "multiple-choice answer selection" appears as a step_0 frame in g5096, g5100, and g5276) even though none of the underlying problems are about socks or multiple choice — so B's stock templates are thematic rather than verbatim, and get overwritten by more nearly-correct arithmetic later in training on many (not all) problems.

## 4. Trajectory over training

**Reader A**: flat, and flat at the floor. Topicality and value recovery are zero at step 0 and remain zero at step 300 for every one of the 20 annotated problems — I found no case where a later checkpoint discusses the actual problem when an earlier one did not, or vice versa. The only step-wise change is stylistic homogenization: step_0 draws on a broader variety of non-math registers (sports polls, casino reviews, a bingo FAQ) while steps 100–300 collapse toward a narrower "OLS regression coefficient" register across most problems. This is a change in surface style, not in grounding.

**Reader B**: improves for a majority of problems, flat for a minority, and degrades outright for one. Clear step0→step300 improvement: g5148 (ungrounded 88/3≈29.33 at step_0 → exact "45÷3=15" at 100/200/300), g5544 (a socks/Python tangent at step_0 → near-miss "7×17=119" at 100/200 → exact "7×18=126" at step_300), g5672 (a retail-listing tangent at step_0 → correct real sub-computation operands "3×8" and "2×5" from step_100 on), g5412 (an unrelated "100×3=300" at step_0 → exact "the answer must be 50" at step_200 and step_300), and g5044 (a generic "60 minutes×5=300" at step_0 → "the expected score is 60," the true answer, at step_200). Flat-and-wrong across all four steps: g5052 (never surfaces 450/150/15/135) and g5276 (consistently off by roughly a factor of 10 at every step, from step_0's unrelated multiple-choice frame through step_300's "24 students" against a true total of 240). Outright degradation: g5324, whose text is a normal-looking (if wrong) word-problem narrative at step_0 but collapses into a mechanically repeated fraction/addition sequence from step_100 through step_300, i.e. loses coherence rather than gaining or losing topicality.

## 5. Verdict scores

**Reader A** — Topicality: 0.0. Justification: across all 80 annotated texts, none references the subject matter of its problem; every text is cast as academic/statistical or consumer-review prose (regression coefficients, poll percentages, casino RTP tables). Value recovery: 0.0. Justification: no gold intermediate or gold-answer number from any of the 20 problems' gold_chain/gold_answer appears anywhere in A's text; A's numbers (percentages, decimal coefficients, table values) are generic genre exemplars. Stability: 0.7. Justification: every one of the 20 problems stays in exactly one of two coarse registers (statistical-narrative or tabular) across all four of its own steps — never switching mid-problem — even though the specific fabricated statistic changes step to step and the register itself is unrelated to the problem's content.

**Reader B** — Topicality: 0.3. Justification: B is reliably in the correct *meta-genre* (a grade-school arithmetic word problem being solved), but it almost never uses the real cover-story nouns (marbles, restaurants, lego blocks, apples become coins, socks, eggs, fish tanks), and a few instances break even the meta-genre entirely (g5544 step_0 into code, g5672 step_0 into a product listing, g5368 step_0 into circle geometry). Value recovery: 0.4. Justification: a clear minority-but-substantial fraction of problems produce an exact gold intermediate or gold-answer digit string somewhere in their four steps (e.g. "the expected score is 60" for g5044, "45 ÷ 3 = 15" for g5148, "7 × 18 = 126 eggs" for g5544, "the answer must be 500 - 150 - 300 - 200 = 50" for g5412, "the answer must be '10'" for g5068), but these correct numbers are usually embedded in an otherwise wrong or invented computation, and several problems (g5052, g5276, g5324) never surface a correct value at any step. Stability: 0.3. Justification: the cover story changes almost every step within a given problem (a single problem might be narrated as coins, then tickets, then a test score across its four checkpoints), so surface narrative stability is low; the partial counterweight is that once a real operand surfaces it often recurs for the remaining steps (45/3/15 in g5148, 3×8 in g5672, 6/12 in g5044 and g5612), which keeps the score above the floor rather than at it.
